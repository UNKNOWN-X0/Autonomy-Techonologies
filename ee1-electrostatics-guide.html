1.1–1.3 Charge, Coulomb’s law, field strength

Charge QQQ is measured in coulomb, 1 C=1 As1\,\mathrm{C}=1\,\mathrm{As}1C=1As. Like charges repel, unlike charges attract.

  • Triboelectric effect: rubbing separates charges, it does not create them. Glass rubbed with wool loses electrons (+++); plastic gains electrons (−-−). The sign convention glass =+=+=+ is arbitrary.

  • Every charge is a multiple of the elementary charge e≈1.602⋅10−19 Ce\approx1.602\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}e≈1.602⋅10−19C. Proton +e+e+e, electron −e-e−e; a neutral atom has Nprotons=NelectronsN_\text{protons}=N_\text{electrons}Nprotons​=Nelectrons​.

  • Charge conservation: the total charge of a closed system is constant; charges appear or vanish only in ±\pm± pairs.

Field lines [1.7]

A field line is a curve whose tangent at every point is the field vector.

  • Field lines start on positive charges and end on negative charges.

  • They meet conductor surfaces at right angles.

  • They never cross, except at field-free points: a nonzero field has a unique direction.

  • Line density indicates field magnitude when no vectors are drawn.

  • Typical pictures: radial out of +Q+Q+Q, radial into −Q-Q−Q, parallel and homogeneous between charged plates, curved from +++ to −-− for a dipole, a field-free point midway between two equal charges.

Uniqueness theorem: a solution of Maxwell’s equations that satisfies the boundary conditions is the only one. Same boundary conditions mean same field.

1.8–1.9 Potential, equipotentials, voltage

  • The electrostatic field is a source field (charges are its sources), like the gravitational field.

Equipotential lines [1.8.1]

  • An equipotential surface is all points with equal potential; its cut with the drawing plane is an equipotential line.

  • Equipotentials are perpendicular to field lines (E⃗⋅ds⃗=0\vec{E}\cdot\mathrm d\vec{s}=0E⋅ds=0 along them). Moving along one costs no work.

  • Equal potential steps between lines: dense lines mean strong field.

  • Conductors in electrostatics: the whole body has one constant potential, the surface is an equipotential, field lines hit it perpendicularly, and the inside is field-free. Reason: any tangential E⃗\vec{E}E would push free charges until it vanishes.

Voltage [1.9]

Voltage is a potential difference and does not depend on the reference point.

U12=φe(P1)−φe(P2)=∫P1P2E⃗⋅ds⃗U_{12}=\varphi_e(P_1)-\varphi_e(P_2)=\int_{P_1}^{P_2}\vec{E}\cdot\mathrm d\vec{s}U12​=φe​(P1​)−φe​(P2​)=∫P1​P2​​E⋅ds

1.10 Electric flux density and Gauss’s law

The flux density D⃗\vec{D}D (displacement field) captures the effect of matter; in linear media it is proportional to E⃗\vec{E}E. Unit As/m2^22 === C/m2^22.

D⃗=εE⃗=ε0εrE⃗,Ψ=∬AD⃗⋅dA⃗[As]\vec{D}=\varepsilon\vec{E}=\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r\vec{E},\qquad \Psi=\iint_A\vec{D}\cdot\mathrm d\vec{A}\quad[\mathrm{As}]D=εE=ε0​εr​E,Ψ=∬A​D⋅dA[As]

  • εr=1\varepsilon_r=1εr​=1 in vacuum and, approximately, in air.

  • dA⃗=n⃗ dA\mathrm d\vec{A}=\vec{n}\,\mathrm dAdA=ndA, with n⃗\vec{n}n the unit normal of the surface element.

Conductor: electrostatic induction [1.12.1, 1.12.3]

  • Free electrons move towards the positive plate, leaving a deficit on the far side. Separate the two halves inside the field and both stay charged.

  • Charges move until the whole body is one equipotential and the inside is field-free.

  • The induced surface charge equals the incident flux density: σF=D\sigma_F=DσF​=D at each surface point; DDD then jumps to 000 inside.

  • A cavity in a conductor is shielded from external fields. A charge placed inside a hollow conducting sphere induces −Q-Q−Q on the inner wall and +Q+Q+Q on the outer wall.

  • Induction is why general field problems with conductors are hard to compute.

Dielectric: polarization [1.12.2, 1.12.4]

An insulator has no free charges, but the charges inside atoms and molecules shift or rotate.

  • Displacement polarization: electron clouds or ions are shifted (electronic, ionic).

  • Orientation polarization: existing dipoles (such as water molecules) turn into the field; a homogeneous field exerts a torque on a dipole.

  • Dipole moment p⃗=Qd⃗\vec{p}=Q\vec{d}p​=Qd, with d⃗\vec{d}d pointing from −-− to +++. Polarization P⃗=1V∑np⃗n\vec{P}=\frac1V\sum_n\vec{p}_nP=V1​∑n​p​n​ (dipole moment per volume).

  • Vacuum and air: εr=1\varepsilon_r=1εr​=1, χ=0\chi=0χ=0.

  • Bound polarization charges appear on the slab surfaces and weaken the field inside; free charges sit on the plates.

1.13–1.14 Capacitors, capacitance, stored energy

A capacitor is two conductors carrying +Q+Q+Q and −Q-Q−Q; all flux runs from one to the other. Charge and voltage are proportional, and the constant is the capacitance. Unit farad, 1 F=1 As/V1\,\mathrm F=1\,\mathrm{As/V}1F=1As/V.

C=QU=∮AD⃗⋅dA⃗∫sE⃗⋅ds⃗C=\frac{Q}{U}=\frac{\oint_A\vec{D}\cdot\mathrm d\vec{A}}{\int_s\vec{E}\cdot\mathrm d\vec{s}}C=UQ​=∫s​E⋅ds∮A​D⋅dA​

The surface integral wraps one electrode; the path runs from one electrode to the other. (German: capacitance === Kapazität, capacitor === Kondensator.)

Recipe for any geometry:

  1. Put ±Q\pm Q±Q on the electrodes.

  2. Find D⃗\vec{D}D with Gauss’s law, then E⃗=D⃗/ε\vec{E}=\vec{D}/\varepsilonE=D/ε.

  3. Integrate E⃗\vec{E}E from one electrode to the other to get UUU.

  4. C=Q/UC=Q/UC=Q/U; QQQ must cancel.

Exam checklist and common pitfalls

Tick each skill once you can do it without notes.

Pitfalls:

  • The vector r⃗\vec{r}r points from the source charge to the observation point, not the other way.

  • E∝1/r2E\propto1/r^2E∝1/r2 but φe∝1/r\varphi_e\propto1/rφe​∝1/r. Do not mix them.

  • WeW_eWe​ in these notes is energy supplied from outside; moving against the force is positive.

  • U12=φ1−φ2=∫P1P2E⃗⋅ds⃗U_{12}=\varphi_1-\varphi_2=\int_{P_1}^{P_2}\vec{E}\cdot\mathrm d\vec{s}U12​=φ1​−φ2​=∫P1​P2​​E⋅ds; swapping indices flips the sign.

  • Only path components along E⃗\vec{E}E contribute to a line integral.

  • Gauss’s law gives D⃗\vec{D}D (the enclosed free charge); divide by ε\varepsilonε for E⃗\vec{E}E.

  • At an interface DnD_nDn​ and EtE_tEt​ are continuous (no surface charge); EnE_nEn​ and DtD_tDt​ jump.

  • The inside of a conductor is field-free in electrostatics; field lines meet its surface at 90∘90^\circ90∘.

  • Plate capacitor: the field outside the plates is zero; each sheet alone gives only σA/2\sigma_A/2σA​/2.

  • Fixed QQQ (disconnected) versus fixed UUU (connected to a source) changes how EEE, DDD and WeW_eWe​ respond to a dielectric.

  • Capacitors combine opposite to resistors: parallel adds CCC, series adds 1/C1/C1/C.

  • Coax capacitance uses ln⁡(b/a)\ln(b/a)ln(b/a), not (b−a)(b-a)(b−a).
